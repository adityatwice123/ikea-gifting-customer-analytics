# Findings and Recommended Actions

Numbers come from the cleaned data: 5,881 customers, about $17.4M revenue, December 2009 to December 2011.

## What we found
- **Revenue is concentrated.** Champions are 22% of customers (1,291) and bring in about 68% of revenue ($11.8M). Their average value is $9,169, against $429 for Lost customers.
- **Half the base has gone quiet.** 50.86% of customers had no purchase in the last 90 days.
- **Q4 carries the year.** Revenue climbs through the year and peaks in November ($2.32M), which fits a gifting business.
- **Churn is not even across time.** The churn rate by order month drops sharply in September to November, when customers buy for the holidays.

## Segment actions
| Segment | Customers | Revenue | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Champions | 1,291 | $11.8M | Protect them. Early access to Q4 ranges, a named account contact for the biggest wholesale buyers, no heavy discounting. |
| Loyal | 1,416 | $2.7M | Move the best of them toward Champions with bundle offers and a loyalty tier. |
| At Risk | 821 | $1.6M | Highest return on effort. They bought often before. Send a win-back message with a personal reason to return, ahead of Q4. |
| New | 442 | $0.4M | Get the second order. A follow-up within 30 days with a related product. |
| Needs Attention | 387 | $0.2M | Low spend and low frequency. Cheap automated campaigns only. |
| Lost | 1,524 | $0.7M | Low value per customer. One low cost reactivation attempt, then stop spending on them. |

## Limits
- The 90 day churn rule is a fixed threshold, not a modelled one.
- Wholesale and retail buyers are not separated, so some Champions are businesses.
- Historical data only, no promotion calendar.
